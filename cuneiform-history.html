The History of Cuneiform

3000 Years of Clay Records
◆ Interactive Timeline of Cuneiform History ◆
c. 3400 BCE
Pre-Cuneiform Proto-Writing
Early pictographic symbols appear on clay tokens and tablets in Uruk.
c. 3200 BCE
First Cuneiform Signs
The wedge-shaped impressions begin to emerge in southern Mesopotamia.
c. 2900 BCE
Sumerian Classical Period
Writing becomes widespread for administration, literature, and religious texts.
c. 2500 BCE
Gilgamesh Era
The Epic of Gilgamesh composed in cuneiform script.
c. 2350 BCE
Akadian Adoption
Sargon's empire adopts cuneiform for Akkadian language.
c. 1800 BCE
Babylonian Mastery
Hammurabi's code inscribed in cuneiform on stone stele.
c. 1200 BCE
Assyrian Library
Ashurbanipal's library at Nineveh holds 30,000+ cuneiform tablets.
c. 600 BCE
Persian Empire
Cuneiform used for administrative purposes across the empire.
c. 500 BCE
Decline Begins
Aramaic becomes dominant; cuneiform limited to scholarly use.
c. 100 BCE
Last Scribes
Final astronomical and ritual texts written in cuneiform.
1850s CE
Modern Decipherment
Henry Rawlinson cracks the cuneiform code using the Behistun Inscription.
1900s CE
Systematic Excavation
Thousands of tablets recovered from archaeological sites.
2020s CE
Digital Preservation
Electronic databases and 3D scanning preserve tablet contents.

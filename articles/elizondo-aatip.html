The Resignation That Shook the Pentagon

Luis Elizondo, former head of the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP), resigned from the Pentagon in 2017 citing excessive secrecy and lack of serious treatment of UAP as national security threats.

AATIP's True Mission

Officially, AATIP studied "advanced aerospace threats." Unofficially, the program investigated craft demonstrating "engineering capabilities far beyond any known human technology." Elizondo confirmed studying materials with "atomic structures that should not exist naturally."

"These objects are displaying flight characteristics that, if you were to apply as a physics problem, break down at the quantum level of engineering." — Luis Elizondo, Former AATIP Director

The $22 Million Secret

AATIP operated from 2007-2012 with a $22 million budget funded through a contract with Bigelow Aerospace. Elizondo revealed the program had "more than enough evidence to justify serious scientific investigation."

NYT Disclosure

In a groundbreaking New York Times article, Elizondo revealed that the Pentagon had been studying UAP for decades, with "multiple videos of unidentified aerial phenomena" in the possession of Defense Department officials.

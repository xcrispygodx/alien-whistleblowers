November 14, 2004

While conducting training exercises 100 miles off the coast of San Diego, the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group detected unidentified aerial contacts on multiple sensors.

The Video Evidence

Commander David Fravor launched an F/A-18F Super Hornet to investigate. The now-famous "Tic Tac" video shows an object approximately 40 feet long, with no visible propulsion system, accelerating from 0 to 40,000 feet in less than a second.

"It was moving faster than anything I've seen. No wings, no exhaust, no control surfaces. Just smooth acceleration in any direction it chose." — Commander David Fravor, US Navy

The Chase Sequence

The UAP demonstrated impossible maneuverability, including instantaneous direction changes. When Fravor's wingman looped around, the object vanished - only to reappear moments later at the CAP point where they had been circling.

Aftermath Suppression

Despite multiple witness accounts and sensor confirmations, the incident was classified and buried. Pilots were told to "drop it" and "focus on the mission." It wasn't until 2017 that the videos were officially released.
